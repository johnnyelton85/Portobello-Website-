Harm Register & Risk Procedure

How we find harm, decide what matters, put controls in place, and keep the register alive. Built for owner-operated plumbing in occupied and tenanted Auckland homes — ladder roof access, small service trenches, underfloor and ceiling work, and occasional hot work.

Draft for review. Issued by Portobello Plumbing Company Limited (NZBN 9429053083524). Ratings are typical for this type of work, not a substitute for the check you do on the day. Residual risk assumes the listed controls are actually used. Add or retire rows as the work changes.

1. Purpose

This procedure meets our duty to identify hazards, assess risks, and eliminate those risks so far as is reasonably practicable — or minimise them where elimination is not reasonably practicable (HSWA s30 and the General Risk and Workplace Management Regulations).

2. When we assess risk

  • Trade-level: this Harm Register, reviewed at least annually.
  • Job-level: a site check on arrival, and a documented check before higher-risk tasks (ladder roof work, asbestos uncertainty, small excavation, underfloor/ceiling, hot work, heavy lifts, live services).
  • Change: new plant, a different method, a new type of work, or after an incident or near miss.

3. Procedure

  1. Identify what could harm workers or other people: the task, the place, the plant, the substances, the people (including occupants and the public), and the environment.
  2. Assess using the matrix below. Score the risk before extra controls if you are deciding whether the job method is acceptable, and again as residual risk with controls in place.
  3. Control using the hierarchy: eliminate → substitute → isolate / engineer → administer (permits, training, procedures) → PPE. Do not jump to PPE because it is easy.
  4. Do the work only if residual risk is acceptable (Low or Medium with controls used). Extreme residual risk is not acceptable. High residual risk needs Director approval and a tighter method, or the job is deferred.
  5. Monitor and review. If the site changes, stop and reassess. Update this register when a control fails or a new harm appears.

Anyone may stop work if they reasonably believe serious risk exists. Stopping work is not a disciplinary matter.

4. Risk matrix

Likelihood: 1 Rare · 2 Unlikely · 3 Possible · 4 Likely · 5 Almost certain. Consequence: 1 Minor (first aid) · 2 Moderate (medical treatment) · 3 Serious (lost time / hospital) · 4 Major (permanent harm) · 5 Catastrophic (fatality / multiple serious). Score = Likelihood × Consequence.

Score Rating Action
1–4 Low Proceed with standard controls. Keep tidy and watch for change.
5–9 Medium Apply listed controls before starting. Brief everyone on site.
10–16 High Documented site assessment. Extra controls or a different method. Director aware.
17–25 Extreme Do not start. Eliminate or redesign. Seek specialist support if needed.

5. Site check (use on arrival)

  • Safe access, parking, animals, children and other occupants?
  • Known asbestos, leak history, or previous repairs disclosed?
  • Electricity, gas, water isolation points identified?
  • Height, roof condition, weather?
  • Underground or concealed services if we will cut or dig?
  • Manual handling — cylinder, bath, pipe, debris?
  • Hygiene, sewage, sharps, underfloor or ceiling space?
  • Emergency exit, phone coverage, and Rosie check-in for lone work?
  • Hot work today — permit, extinguisher, combustibles, tenants?

If the answer to a risk question is “not sure”, treat it as present until you know otherwise — especially asbestos and live services.

H01 Working at height — ladders, roofs, ceilings. Falls, dropped tools, fragile roofing. Workers, occupants below 20 Eliminate height where we can work from the ground. Ladder only, following WorkSafe guidance: stable, three points of contact, tied or footed. No roof work in high wind, rain or on fragile cladding. Keep people out of the drop zone. H02 Asbestos — AC pipe, gaskets, vinyl, soffits, textured coatings in pre-2000 buildings. Inhaled fibres. Workers, occupants 20 Assume present until ruled out. If accidentally disturbed: isolate area, wet-clean only as advised, do not sweep dry, notify the customer / property manager and the Director. 6

H04 Electricity near water — shock, arc, secondary fall. Workers, occupants 20 Isolate power when fittings or wet areas create a shock path. Licensed electrician for electrical work. Emergency stop and notify if a service is hit. 8

H07 Hot water / heat — scalds, burns from cylinders, pipework, soldering, steam. Workers, occupants 12 Isolate and depressurise before opening systems. Warn occupants. Gloves and eye protection. Check tempering / thermostat settings on completion. Allow cooling time. 4

H09 Silica and dust — cutting tiles, concrete, plaster. Respiratory disease. Workers, occupants 16 Avoid dry cutting. Water suppression or on-tool extraction. P2 (or better) respirator when dust cannot be eliminated. Isolate the room and clean with vacuum, not dry sweeping. 6
H10 Power tools and plant — grinders, drills, drain machines, pressure equipment. Cuts, entanglement, eye injury, noise, stored energy. Workers 12 Guards on, right disc/bit, eye and hearing protection. Loose clothing and jewellery clear of rotating plant. Depressurise before opening lines. Tag out damaged gear. 6
H11 Slips, trips, falls on the level — water, tools, offcuts, poor lighting. Workers, occupants 10 Manage water as we work. Housekeeping, lighting, mats, signage. Clear a path for occupants. Tidy as we go — the Portobello standard. 4
H12 Underfloor and ceiling spaces — restricted access, heat, unseen services, poor air. We do not enter tanks or manholes. 600 mm trenches are not treated as confined space. Workers 12 Underfloor and ceiling only. Check access, lighting, vermin, insulation, heat and cables. Phone check-in with Rosie. Abort if air quality or access is poor. Tanks, manholes and any space that meets the confined-space definition are out of remit. 8
H13 Driving and vehicles — road crash, poorly secured load, reversing on driveways. Workers, public 15 Licensed, roadworthy vehicle. Loads restrained. No phone use while driving. Fatigue management. Reversing with care around children and driveways. 6
H14 Occupants, children, pets and the public — contact with tools, open trenches, curiosity around the work. Others 12 Brief the customer. Barrier or close the work area. Cover openings. Never leave hazardous plant unattended. Stop if people will not stay clear. 4
H15 Sharps / needle stick — gardens, underfloor, public areas, bathrooms. Workers 10 Look before reaching. Tongs/puncture-resistant container if found. Do not recap. Wash and seek medical advice the same day after a stick. 6
H16 Weather and heat — roof work, dehydration, UV, wind, wet surfaces. Workers 10 Reschedule unsafe weather. Sun protection, water, rest. No roof work when wind or rain makes footing unreliable. 4
H17 Lone work and psychosocial — delayed help, fatigue, aggressive or impaired occupants. Workers 10 Share location and expected finish with Rosie (020 407 89812). Phone charged. H18 Gas on a customer site — we do not carry out gasfitting. Harm if we disturb a gas installation or stay in a leak. Fire, explosion, CO. Workers, occupants 20 Out of scope: no gasfitting, appliance install or service. If a leak or gas work is identified, stop, eliminate ignition sources, evacuate if needed, call 111 / the network emergency number, and refer to a licensed gasfitter. Do not isolate or reconnect gas unless it is an immediate life-safety action we are competent to take. 4
H19 Hot work — solder, braze, grind. Fire, burns, sparks in occupied or tenanted homes. Workers, occupants 15 Prefer a cold method. Complete the Hot Work Permit. Clear combustibles, protect surfaces, ventilate. Fire extinguisher at the work point. Occupants and pets clear. Fire-watch after the last flame or grind. Follow a principal’s permit if it is stricter. 6

7. Residual risk we will not accept

  • Roof work that needs a harness, scaffold or roof platform, or any height work in weather or on cladding we cannot control.
  • Disturbing suspected asbestos to “just get it done”.
  • Trenches larger than about 600 mm × 600 mm, or digging where services are unknown and cannot be located or hand-dug safely.
  • Tanks, manholes or any true confined space.
  • Live electrical work, or plumbing work that creates a known shock path.
  • Gasfitting, gas-appliance work, or licensed drainlaying.
  • Hot work without a completed permit and a fire extinguisher at the work point.

8. Review and approval

The Director reviews this register at least annually and after any notifiable event or repeated near miss. New rows can be added on a job and folded into this controlled version at the next review.
