2. Scope of this plan

This plan covers all Portobello work, plant and vehicles. It sits under the Company Health & Safety Policy and is used with the Harm Register / Risk Management Procedure. Where a principal contractor or building site has its own plan, we follow the stricter of the two requirements and coordinate before starting.

3. Roles and responsibilities

Director (Officer and typically the person on the tools)

  • Set and resource the health and safety system.
  • Maintain licences, insurance, plant and training records.
  • Complete or review site risk checks for higher-risk work.
  • Stop work that cannot be done safely.
  • Notify WorkSafe of notifiable events and preserve the scene when required.
  • Review incidents, the Harm Register and this plan.

Workers and contractors (when engaged)

  • Work only within competence and licence conditions.
  • Follow this plan, site rules and agreed controls.
  • Use plant and PPE as intended and report defects.
  • Report hazards, incidents and near misses the same day.

Customers / occupiers / other PCBUs

  • Provide safe, reasonable access and disclose known hazards (asbestos, services, previous repairs, animals, occupants).
  • Keep children, visitors and animals clear of the work area.
  • Consult, cooperate and coordinate activities that could affect each other.

4. Risk management

Risk management follows Document D. In short:

  1. Identify hazards for the trade (Harm Register) and for the specific site.
  2. Assess likelihood and consequence.
  3. Apply the hierarchy of controls — eliminate first, then minimise.
  4. Do not start high-risk tasks until controls are in place.
  5. Review after incidents, near misses, new plant or a change of method.

A dynamic site check is completed at arrival: access, occupants, pets, weather, services, working at height, asbestos indicators, isolation needs and emergency exits. If conditions change, we reassess.

5. Training, competence and induction

Plumbing, drainlaying and gasfitting work is only done by people holding the correct registration, or by trainees under the supervision required by the Plumbers, Gasfitters and Drainlayers Act.

Minimum competence we maintain or require:

  • Current PGDB practising licence(s) for the work being done.
  • Driver licence appropriate to the vehicle.
  • First aid — at least one trained person available on typical jobs (to confirm current certificate).
  • Working at height awareness for ladder and roof work.
  • Asbestos awareness for work in pre-2000 buildings.
  • Site-specific induction on controlled building sites (Site Safe or principal’s induction as required).

New workers or contractors are inducted on this plan, the Harm Register, emergency contacts, incident reporting, PPE and the right to stop work before they start on a live job.

6. Plant, tools, vehicles and equipment

  • Vehicles are roadworthy, loaded so that tools cannot become projectiles, and not driven while fatigued or under the influence of drugs or alcohol.
  • Ladders, harnesses (if used), electrical tools, drain machines and pressure equipment are checked before use. Damaged gear is tagged out.
  • Electrical tools used on site are in-test where a residual current device and inspection regime is required by the site or by the nature of the work.
  • Hot water cylinders, baths and other heavy items are moved with a planned lift, trolley or two-person method — not a rushed one-person strain.
  • Plant is used only for its intended purpose and within manufacturer limits.

7. Hazardous substances

Typical products include drain chemicals, solvents, adhesives, solders, flux, water-treatment media and fuels. We will:

  • keep a simple inventory and current safety data sheets;
  • store products upright, labelled and away from occupants and ignition sources;
  • use the smallest practical quantity and the least-hazardous product that will do the job;
  • wear the PPE specified on the SDS; and
  • not mix incompatible chemicals (especially acids and oxidisers, or products that release toxic gas).

8. Personal protective equipment

PPE is the last line of defence, not the first. Standard site kit includes sturdy footwear, eye protection, gloves suited to the task, hearing protection for noisy plant, and a hi-vis garment when working near traffic or on a construction site. Extra PPE is used for the task: P2 or higher respiratory protection for silica/dust or sewage aerosols, chemical gloves and eye protection for drain chemicals, cut-resistant gloves for sheet metal and roofing, and sun protection for roof work. PPE is maintained, replaced when worn, and not a substitute for isolation or a safer method.

9. High-risk work we commonly face

Working at height

Ladders, roofs, ceilings and scaffolding. Prefer work from the ground or a stable platform. Three points of contact on ladders. No roof work in high wind, rain or on fragile cladding unless a safe system is in place.

Asbestos

Assume asbestos may be present in Auckland homes built or renovated before 2000 (pipes, gaskets, vinyl, soffits, textured coatings). Do not drill, cut or disturb suspect material. Stop and arrange a competent survey or licensed removal if needed.

Services and excavation

Before digging or cutting, ask the customer about known services, review available plans and use locating or careful hand excavation. Treat plans as indicative only.

Biological hazards

Wastewater, blocked drains and underfloor work. Gloves, eye protection, hygiene, cover cuts, and keep occupants clear. Vaccination status (e.g. tetanus) is encouraged.

Electricity and water

Isolate power where work could contact electrical fittings or create a shock path. Do not work on live electrical equipment. Engage a licensed electrician when required.

Lone work and occupied homes

Tell someone the job location and expected finish. Reassess if an occupant is aggressive, impaired or the site feels unsafe. Keep children and pets out of the work area.

10. Emergency preparedness

Situation Immediate action
Serious injury / medical emergency Call 111. First aid. Do not move the person unless they are in immediate danger. Notify the Director.
Fire Evacuate, call 111, do not re-enter. Isolate gas/electricity only if safe to do so.
Gas leak (if present) Evacuate, eliminate ignition sources, ventilate if safe, call 111 / gas emergency number. Do not operate electrical switches in the affected area.
Service strike (power, gas, fibre) Stop work, keep people back, call the relevant emergency / network number, treat as live until confirmed otherwise.
Flooding / uncontrolled water Isolate supply at the nearest safe valve. Keep electrical hazards in mind. Protect occupants and property if safe.
Needle stick / sewage exposure Encourage bleeding of puncture wounds, wash, seek medical advice the same day.

11. Incident, near miss and notifiable events

All injuries, near misses, property damage and dangerous occurrences are recorded the same day. The Director reviews the record, identifies causes and updates the Harm Register if needed.

A notifiable event (death, notifiable injury or illness, or notifiable incident as defined in HSWA) is reported to WorkSafe as soon as possible by the fastest means, then in writing within 48 hours where required. The scene is preserved until authorised, except to help the injured, make the area safe or prevent further harm.

Injured workers are supported to get treatment and a safe return to work. We do not victimise anyone for reporting a concern or refusing unsafe work.

12. Worker engagement

In a small crew, engagement is direct: daily discussion of the job, a standing invitation to raise issues, and a review of any incident with the people involved. If the business grows past the small-PCBU threshold for formal representation, we will put worker participation practices in place as required by HSWA.

13. Contractors, visitors and overlapping duties

Before engaging a contractor we check competence, licences and insurance appropriate to the task, and share this plan and relevant Harm Register entries. On a principal-controlled site we attend the required induction and follow site rules for permits, isolation and amenities. We coordinate with electricians, builders, roofers and other trades so one trade’s work does not put another at risk.

14. Health monitoring and wellbeing

We watch for hearing, respiratory, skin and musculoskeletal issues linked to the work. Fatigue, heat on roofs, and the mental load of lone work and difficult sites are treated as health risks. Anyone impaired by alcohol, drugs or extreme fatigue does not work.

15. Records we keep

  • This plan, the Policy and the Harm Register (current version).
  • Licences, training and induction records.
  • Plant / vehicle checks and residual-current / test tags where used.
  • Hazardous substance inventory and SDS.
  • Site risk checks for higher-risk jobs.
  • Incident and near-miss reports, investigations and corrective actions.
  • WorkSafe notifications (if any).

Records are kept for at least the period required by law, and longer if they support an insurance or liability matter.

16. Review

The Director reviews this plan at least annually, and after a notifiable event, a repeated near miss, a change in the type of work, or a change in legislation that affects us. Version control is by date on the cover block. Previous versions are archived, not overwritten without a record.
